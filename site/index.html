LORA FLOWTRON DATAMASTER 3000 SERIAL MODEM - LFD3K-SM

Dial a BBS across town. No phone line.

A Hayes-style modem that talks over LoRa radio and Wi-Fi. Plug a Heltec V4 or V3 into any terminal, type ATDT, and you get RINGING, CONNECT, retrains and NO CARRIER, the way BBS calling used to feel. The call goes over the air on 918.5 MHz. It is the radio side of the AXIS BBS.

LFD3K-SM

A real call, over the air

Recorded live on 2026-11-12. The PC types into a Heltec V3, the V3 dials a Heltec V4 over LoRa on 918.5 MHz (it trained at SF5, 62,500 bps on the air, with ARQ and LZ compression), and the V4 answers and bridges the call over Wi-Fi into the AXIS BBS. Then a walk around the board: log in, read a message, look at a file area, the system stats, the ANSI pack viewer, and log off. Real time, nothing sped up: left is the caller's screen, right is the V4's own terminal.

Why it rules

Ten reasons. Full details live in the features log and the design doc.

Real modem manners

ATD, ATA, ATH, ATO, ATI, AT&V, ATZ, S registers, +++ with guard time, verbose and numeric result codes, auto-answer. Your old terminal scripts just work.

8 speed tiers, picked live

SF12 to SF5 at 500 kHz, about 1.2 to 62.5 kbps on the air. The modems train on the measured SNR, start at the best tier the link can hold, step down when the signal fades, and climb back up when it clears. You see it happen: RETRAIN.

Nothing gets lost

Selective-repeat ARQ (window 8, SACK bitmap), half-duplex burst manners, keepalives, resync to SF12 when the link dies for a moment, and NO CARRIER only when it is really gone.

LZ compression on the air

Each frame is squeezed against a preset tuned for ANSI art, CP437 boxes and blocks, so BBS screens fly. The CONNECT line says so: /ARQ/LZ, and the DC light comes on.

Wi-Fi too

Its own WPA2 hotspot, a client network, up to 8 named forwards (ATDT AXIS opens a telnet call), and a published forward so radio callers reach a telnet BBS.

Find your friends

Beacons, AT+SCAN, a station directory with pinning, dial by name, and a privacy mode (ON, QUIET or HIDDEN).

A full-screen setup menu

AT+MENU opens an 80x24 ANSI lightbar menu (CP437, 16 colors) for SyncTERM: directory and scan, live link status, settings, forwards, Wi-Fi. See the screens.

A front panel with attitude

The OLED shows modem lights, the call state, tier, RSSI and SNR, a radar sweep while scanning, and an idle show in 17 languages with 12 transitions. See the panel.

Four modes in one radio

On the V4, a custom bootloader and picker carry LFD3K-SM, Simian Radar, RNode and Meshtastic in their own slots: AT+MODE=RNODE and the board comes back up as an RNode. The four modes.

Tested hard

A no_std, no-heap Rust core with a seeded LoRa channel simulator, two-modem call scenarios (SNR dips, lost frames, collisions, resyncs) and proptest fuzzing of frames and AT lines. The simulator found real bugs before any radio did.

The tier ladder

Eight LoRa tiers at 500 kHz bandwidth, coding rate 4/5. Air rates, fastest first. The CONNECT string always shows the real tier, for example CONNECT LORA SF7 BW500 21875/ARQ/LZ.

Animations

The front panel never sits still. These are the real renderer's frames, drawn by the same pure C code the firmware runs, previewed on the PC. Click any of them to see it big.

The setup menu

AT+MENU opens a full-screen 80x24 ANSI lightbar menu. Best in SyncTERM with the CP437 font. Up, Down and Tab move, Enter opens or edits, a letter jumps, Esc goes back. Every edit is checked the same way the AT command checks it. These screens are rendered from the menu code by the PC preview tool.

The front panel

A 0.96 inch 128x64 OLED drawn at up to 20 fps by its own task, with a hand-made 5x7 font. Top row: eight lights, OH CD SD RD HS EC DC AA. Middle: the state or the tier with an eight cell ladder. Bottom: station name, RSSI and SNR with bars. Mockups come from the same pure drawing function the firmware uses; the ones marked real panel are captures from the board.

Four modes, one radio

On the Heltec V4 a small picker starts first on every boot. Do nothing and it starts the mode you used last. From inside the modem, one command switches. Each firmware has its own flash slot, so updating one never touches the others. The guide is in Multimode. The V3 runs the modem alone.

On the air

Checked with an SDR. Every ATDT makes a burst on 918.5 MHz, one per RINGING, each about 0.3 s long (the SF12 call request airtime), about 510 kHz wide and centred on 918.50 MHz.

Quick start

Any terminal at 115200 baud. For the menu, SyncTERM with ANSI-BBS emulation and the CP437 font. On a Heltec V3, open the port with DTR and RTS off.

ATI                      who am I (name, version, release)
AT+MENU                  the full-screen setup menu
AT+SCAN                  look for other modems on the air
ATDT AXISMODEM-1A2B      dial another modem by name over LoRa
ATDT AXIS                dial a saved forward (a telnet BBS) over Wi-Fi
ATDT bbs.example.com:23  dial any telnet host
+++  (wait)  ATH         hang up
AT&V                     show every setting and the link
AT+MODE=RNODE            come back up as an RNode (V4 multimode)

Every AT command, setting and S register: features, settings and AT commands. No second radio? SimNet lets the PC be the far modem over Wi-Fi, running the same core with real LoRa timing.

The Rust toolchain for the ESP32-S3 is installed with espup. The first firmware build downloads ESP-IDF and takes a while; later builds take seconds.

Hardware

Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V4

ESP32-S3R2, 16 MB flash, SX1262 with the GC1109 front end. Native USB. Runs the modem alone or the four-mode picker.

Heltec WiFi LoRa 32 V3

ESP32-S3, 8 MB flash, SX1262 straight to the antenna (no front end, so S30 is the power at the antenna). Runs the modem alone with the AXIS boot logo at power on. Its USB-C is a CP2102 serial port at 115200: open it with DTR and RTS off. The V3 guide.

918.5 MHz calling channel, 500 kHz bandwidth. Check the rules for your region before you transmit; the default TX power (S30) is low on purpose.
