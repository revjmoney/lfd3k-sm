## Foreword

This specification is an AXIS / LFD3K-SM specification, published by Rev. J. Money as part of the V223.LRA protocol stack. It is not an ITU-T Recommendation.

V223.LRA.90 adds voice calls to the LFD3K-SM data link. A voice call is placed to a **person** (a voice name), not to a station, and rings every device that person has registered at any station in range. Once answered it trains like a data call, carries the two voice applications' scrambler handshake as data, and then switches to fixed time slots that carry sealed voice frames in both directions. The voice applications talk to their modem over the voice host protocol (VHP), also specified here. Encryption is end to end between the applications and is specified in V223.LRA.92; the modem never sees keys or audio.

## 1 Scope

This part specifies frame type 15 of V223.LRA.LINK and all its sub-types; voice names, device labels and registrations; voice beacons and voice scan answers; voice call setup with answer tokens, ringing, decline, busy behaviour and late pickups; the talk plan of each tier (codec, talk mode, frame length, sizes); the voice scheduler (slots, listener slots, the push to talk floor, voice ready, timers); and the voice host protocol between a voice application and its modem.

It does not specify the FLOWTRON SCRAMBLER (V223.LRA.92), the user interface of a voice application, or group channels (a later work item, for which type 15 sub-types 7 and up are reserved).

## 3 Terms and definitions

Terms of V223.LRA.LINK clause 3 apply. In addition:

- **3.1 voice name**: the name of a person: 1 to 16 printable ASCII characters (0x20 to 0x7E) without `:`, compared without regard to ASCII case. Voice names are a namespace of their own: a voice name never matches a station name.
- **3.2 device label**: the name of one of a person's devices: 1 to 8 printable ASCII characters without `:` or `/`, compared without regard to case. A device is written `NAME/LABEL` (split at the last `/`).
- **3.3 voice application (app)**: the program that captures and plays audio, runs the scrambler and talks to a modem over the VHP.
- **3.4 session**: one VHP connection between an app and a modem; a modem has up to 4.
- **3.5 registration**: a voice name and device label an app has registered with its modem over a session.
- **3.6 answer token**: a non-zero 32 bit random number drawn by the device that picks up a voice call; it tells the caller's TierSet which device won.
- **3.7 talk plan**: the codec, talk mode, frame length and largest sealed frame size used on a tier (clause 10).
- **3.8 duplex**: both ends send voice in every slot. **push to talk (PTT)**: only the holder of the floor sends voice.
- **3.9 slot**: a fixed time interval of the talk plan's frame length in which each end may send one frame.
- **3.10 floor**: in push to talk, the right to send voice: Free, held by the caller, or held by the answerer.
- **3.11 sealed voice frame**: an encoded voice frame after V223.LRA.92 sealing; opaque to the modem.
- **3.12 control bytes**: the opaque bytes the two apps exchange over the call's reliable ARQ stream (Annex D).
- **3.13 listen period L**: in push to talk, `listen_every * frame_ms` (clause 11.6).

## 5 Conventions

Clause 5 of V223.LRA.LINK applies (BCP 14 keywords, little endian integers, bit 0 the least significant bit, name fields as a length byte and the bytes). A **label field** is a length byte (0 to 8) and the label bytes; a length of 0 means "no label" where a clause allows it. ## 6 Frame type 15 and its sub-types

### 6.1 Layout

Every voice frame on the air is a V223.LRA.LINK frame of type 15. The first payload byte is the **sub-type**; the rest is the sub-type's body. Header flags SHALL be 0 on every type 15 frame. The header call id, Seq and Ack fields are used as each sub-type below states, and are 0 otherwise.

**Table 1: Type 15 sub-types**

| Sub-type | Name | Call id | Seq | Ack | Body (clause) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Voice beacon | 0 | 0 | 0 | 8.1 |
| 1 | Voice scan answer | 0 | 0 | 0 | 8.3 |
| 2 | Voice call request | call | 0 | 0 | 9.1 |
| 3 | Voice answer | call | 0 | 0 | 9.3 |
| 4 | Voice decline | call | 0 | 0 | empty |
| 5 | Voice data (slot frame) | call | slot number | ARQ ack | 11.4 |
| 6 | Listener (slot frame) | call | slot number, or 0 before the slots | ARQ ack | 11.4 |
| 7 to 255 | (not allocated here) | | | | 7 and up are reserved for group channels; ranges in V223.LRA Annex A.6 |

### 6.2 Receiving

A station SHALL drop a type 15 frame whose payload is empty or whose sub-type it does not implement (V223.LRA.LINK clause 7.5), and SHALL drop a frame whose body does not have the exact shape its clause gives (every parser requires the exact length, valid names and valid labels). A station that does not implement this part drops every type 15 frame; that is why nothing voice can ever be mistaken for data by an older build.

## 7 Voice names, device labels and registrations

- A station SHALL hold at most **4** registrations, one per VHP session (clause 12.2). A second registration on the same session replaces the first.
- A registration lives while its session sends frames and is dropped **30000 ms** after the session's last frame (VOICE_GRACE), or at once when the app unregisters or leaves.
- Registrations SHALL NOT survive a restart of the station.
- A station SHALL NOT list its own registrations among the voice devices it has heard.

## 8 Voice discovery

All frames of this clause are discovery frames: V223.LRA.LINK clause 11.1 (SF12, call id 0, no call, listen before talk, 10 s spacing) applies to them.

### 8.1 Voice beacon (sub-type 0)

| Body offset | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | station | name field: the sender's station name |
| 1 + L | count | u8, 0 to 4: the number of items |
| 2 + L | items | `count` times: a voice name in a name field, then its device label in a label field (length 1 to 8) |

The longest voice beacon is 128 bytes on the air (four 16 byte names with 8 byte labels), about 1.1 s at SF12.

### 8.2 Voice beacon schedule

A station in privacy mode ON with at least one registration SHALL send a voice beacon:

- after each normal Beacon (the 10 s spacing puts it at least 10 s later), except when a voice beacon went out less than **150000 ms** (half the beacon interval) before that Beacon;
- soon after a new or changed registration, at the next moment the discovery rules allow;
- soon after the privacy mode changes to ON while registrations exist.

It SHALL NOT send one while its own scan collects answers, nor in QUIET or HIDDEN. The normal Beacon itself does not change, except that services bit 3 (VOICE, V223.LRA.LINK Table 7) is set while registrations exist.

### 8.3 Voice scan answer (sub-type 1)

| Body offset | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | station | name field: the answering station's name |
| 1 + L | services | u8 (V223.LRA.LINK Table 7) |
| 2 + L | snr | i16: the SNR x4 the ScanReq was heard at |
| 4 + L | scan id | u8: copied from the ScanReq |
| 5 + L | count | u8, 1 to 4 |
| 6 + L | items | as in clause 8.1 |

A station with at least one registration, in ON or QUIET, SHALL answer a ScanReq that carries flag VOICE with a voice scan answer **in place of** its ScanResp, in the same slot and under the same rules (V223.LRA.LINK clause 11.5). Without registrations, or to a ScanReq without flag VOICE, it answers with a normal ScanResp. The longest voice scan answer is 132 bytes. A scanning station SHALL count a voice scan answer as a scan answer.

### 8.4 Learning voice devices (informative)

The reference implementation keeps a voice directory of up to 16 devices (voice name, label, the station they were last heard through, and a best tier from the SNR with the S20 margin; for a scan answer the worse of the two directions), filled from voice beacons and voice scan answers and never from frames carrying its own station name. A user may pin a voice name; in HIDDEN mode only callers whose voice name is pinned ring (clause 9.2), a voice scan answer only refreshes a known device or records a device of a pinned name, while voice beacons still add new devices.

## 9 Voice call setup

### 9.1 Voice call request (sub-type 2)

| Body offset | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | caps | Capability byte (V223.LRA.LINK Table 6). Only bit 0 (CAP_LZ) is read; a receiver SHALL ignore bits 1 to 7 here |
| 1 | station | name field: the caller's station name |
| . | name | name field: the caller's voice name |
| . | label | label field (1 to 8): the caller's device label |
| . | callee | name field: the voice name called |
| . | callee label | label field (0 to 8): one device of the callee, or empty for any device |

The body SHALL be exactly these fields. The longest voice call request is 76 bytes on the air.

To place a voice call a caller with a registration SHALL choose a call id as for a data call (V223.LRA.LINK clause 8.1) and send the voice call request on SF12, and again every PERIOD (3000 ms), until it is bound to an answer (clause 9.4), it is declined, or S7 seconds have passed since the dial (then it sends one Hangup and reports NO ANSWER). It listens on SF12.

### 9.2 Ringing

A station that hears a voice call request computes the set of its registrations it **rings**: those whose voice name equals the callee (ignoring case) and, when the callee label is not empty, whose label equals it, **excluding** the caller's own device (a registration whose label equals the request's caller label at a station whose name equals the request's station). Station names never match a voice call.

- In HIDDEN mode a station SHALL ignore a voice call request whose caller voice name is not pinned.
- A station with any call or call attempt of its own (voice, data, or a non radio call) SHALL ignore the request and send nothing (no CallReject), so the person's other devices keep ringing.
- A station that declined this call id (clause 9.6) SHALL NOT ring again while repeats of it keep arriving less than 7000 ms apart.
- Otherwise, if the set is not empty, an idle station rings those registrations (Ring to their sessions, clause 12) and sends nothing. Repeats of the same call id refresh the ring. The ring ends with no repeat for 7000 ms or after S7.

A station SHALL NOT answer a voice call automatically; only an app (VHP Answer) answers.

### 9.3 Picking up: voice answer and voice Train

The device that picks up SHALL:

1. draw an answer token: a uniform random non-zero 32 bit number, independent per pickup;
2. queue, on SF12, a **voice answer** (sub-type 3) and then a **voice Train**:

| Voice answer body offset | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | token | u32 |
| 4 | station | name field: the answering station's name |
| . | label | label field (1 to 8): the answering device's label |

   The voice Train is the Train of V223.LRA.LINK clause 7.4.3 (3 bytes, or 4 with agreed capabilities) followed by the 4 byte token (7 or 8 bytes);
3. enter *await TierSet* (listening on SF12) and send the answer and Train **at most twice** in all: the second time at `3000 + (token mod 1000)` ms after the first, or, if a repeat of the request arrives after the first went out, at `(token >> 10) mod 700` ms after that repeat if that is sooner;
4. if no TierSet carrying its token arrives within **6000 ms** (`2 * PERIOD`) after its last send, or S7 seconds after picking up, stop **silently** (answered elsewhere).

### 9.4 Binding (caller)

- A caller that is dialing and not yet bound SHALL bind to the token of the first voice answer of its call it hears, record the answering station and label, stop sending requests and wait for the Train. Later answers of other tokens get no reply.
- A voice Train of its call SHALL be accepted only if its token equals the bound token. If the caller is not yet bound (the answer was lost) the first voice Train binds it.
- A caller bound by an answer whose Train does not arrive within **9000 ms** (`3 * PERIOD`) SHALL unbind and start sending requests again (S7 still counts from the dial).
- On the accepted Train the caller proceeds as V223.LRA.LINK clause 8.5, except that the training TierSet carries the tier index followed by the bound token (5 bytes), on every send.
- In a voice call a caller SHALL ignore CallAck and CallReject frames of its call id.

### 9.5 Answered elsewhere

- A ringing device that hears a voice answer or a TierSet of its call SHALL stop ringing silently (answered elsewhere).
- A device in *await TierSet* or *probe* that hears a TierSet of its call with **its** token proceeds as V223.LRA.LINK clause 8.6 (TierAck carries the tier only). A TierSet with another token makes a device that has not yet received its own TierSet stop silently; a device that has already received its own ignores it.
- A device that picked up but has not received its TierSet and hears a Hangup of its call SHALL stop silently.
- Probe, connect and the connected call then run as V223.LRA.LINK clauses 8.7, 8.8, 9 and 10, with the changes of clause 11. Retrain TierSets inside the call are 1 byte, as for data.

Two devices that pick up at the same instant may both be lost to a collision; the caller then keeps sending requests and both ring again. Two devices are never both connected.

### 9.6 Decline (sub-type 4)

A ringing device whose user declines SHALL send the voice decline (empty body) **twice** on SF12 and stop ringing. A caller not yet bound that hears it SHALL end the attempt (DECLINED) without sending anything. A device still ringing for that call that hears it SHALL stop (declined elsewhere). A bound caller ignores it.

### 9.7 Hanging up during setup

A ringing device that hangs up sends nothing. A device that picked up but has not yet received a TierSet with its token SHALL send nothing when it hangs up (a Hangup carries no token and would end the winner's call). Otherwise V223.LRA.LINK clause 10.6 applies.

### 9.8 Compression

Compression is negotiated exactly as for data (the capability byte of the request and the 4th byte of the Train, V223.LRA.CMPRS) and applies to Data frames of the call while it runs in data timing.

## 10 Talk plans and codecs

### 10.1 The talk plan is fixed by the tier

Codec, talk mode and frame length are **never negotiated on the air**. Both ends derive them from the call's current tier with Table 2, so both always agree. A station and an app SHALL use exactly Table 2.

**Table 2: Talk plan per tier (normative)**

| Tier | Codec id | Codec | Mode (id) | frame_ms | Encoded bytes | max_sealed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SF5 | 8 | Opus 10 kbit/s | Duplex (1) | 120 | 150 | 170 |
| SF6 | 2 | Opus 8 kbit/s | Duplex (1) | 200 | 200 | 220 |
| SF7 | 4 | Codec 2 2400 | Duplex (1) | 200 | 60 | 80 |
| SF8 | 5 | Codec 2 1300 | Duplex (1) | 200 | 35 | 55 |
| SF9 | 6 | Codec 2 1200 | Push to talk (2) | 400 | 60 | 80 |
| SF10 | 6 | Codec 2 1200 | Push to talk (2) | 400 | 60 | 80 |
| SF11 | 7 | Codec 2 700C | Push to talk (2) | 1000 | 100 | 120 |
| SF12 | 7 | Codec 2 700C | Push to talk, delayed (3) | 2000 | 200 | 220 |

### 10.3 Encoded voice frame

The encoded voice frame (the plaintext that is sealed) is:

- **Opus** [RFC6716]: mono, 16 kHz sampling, constant bit rate at the codec's rate, application "VoIP". The frame is cut into equal packets of 100 ms when frame_ms is a multiple of 100, else of 20 ms. Each packet is exactly `rate * packet_ms / 8000` bytes (an encoder that returns fewer pads with zero bytes; more are cut); the packets are concatenated with no length prefixes. SF5: six 20 ms packets of 25 bytes; SF6: two 100 ms packets of 100 bytes.
- **Codec 2** [CODEC2]: 8 kHz sampling; `frame_ms / 20` (modes 3200, 2400) or `frame_ms / 40` (modes 1300, 1200, 700C) codec frames, each the whole bytes `codec2_encode` produces for that mode, concatenated in time order. SF7: ten frames of 6 bytes; SF8: five of 7; SF9 and SF10: ten of 6; SF11: twenty five of 4; SF12: fifty of 4.

A decoder SHALL treat a frame whose length is not exactly the encoded size of the current plan as lost. How lost frames are concealed, the sample rate inside the app, and the jitter buffer depth are implementation choices (Annex D).

### 10.4 Plan changes

When the tier changes (a retrain or a resync), the new plan applies from the next slot boundary (clause 11.5). An app SHALL switch codecs when CallState reports the new plan (clause 12.6). A sealed frame longer than the new max_sealed is not sent.

## 11 The voice scheduler

### 11.1 Before the first voice: data timing

From CONNECT a voice call runs on the data timing of V223.LRA.LINK clauses 9 and 10, so the apps' scrambler handshake (control bytes, Annex D) moves at full data speed. Each station learns from its own app when the app is **ready for voice** (VHP VoiceReady, clause 12): the scrambler is up and the user's trust decision is made.

- From the moment its app is ready, a station SHALL cut new control bytes into ARQ chunks of at most `chunk(t)` bytes (Table 5), t being the tier at the time the bytes are queued.
- The **answerer**, once ready and with no outstanding chunk longer than `chunk(t)`, SHALL send a Listener frame (clause 11.4) with flag READY and no chunk, as a turn ending frame, and again every `2 * RTO(t)` ms until the slots start.
- The **caller** is the clock master. When it is ready, has heard a frame with flag READY from the answerer, has no outstanding chunk longer than `chunk(t)`, and holds the turn, it SHALL start the slots at once: that moment is the start of slot 0. Every frame it sends from then on carries flag SLOTS.
- The answerer, once ready, SHALL start its slots when it hears a slot frame with flag SLOTS, and set its slot clock from it (clause 11.5).

Once the slots run they run until the call ends; clauses 9.1, 9.2, 10.1, 10.3 and 10.4 of V223.LRA.LINK are replaced by this clause.

### 11.2 Who may send

Let n be the slot number, `listen_every` and `room` as in Table 5, and the floor owner as in clause 11.7.

- **Duplex**: both ends are due in every slot.
- **Push to talk**: the floor holder is due in every slot. With the floor free both ends are due in every slot. The other end (the listener) is due in **listener slots**, those with `n mod listen_every = 0`. In the slot where the caller announces a floor change it is due as well.
- An end that is not due MAY still send a queued link frame (a retrain TierSet) in a slot where `room` is true.
- **Replies** (a TierAck or a Keepalive reply) SHALL be sent as soon as the hold allows (clause 11.5), without waiting for a sub-slot.

When due, an end sends exactly one slot frame per slot:

1. a **voice data** frame with its oldest queued sealed voice frame, if it may talk (duplex, or it holds the floor), a frame is queued, the frame is not longer than the current plan's max_sealed, and control bytes are not starving (item 3);
2. otherwise a **Listener** frame, carrying the next due ARQ chunk if one is due and fits (its length at most `chunk(t)`, or a longer chunk queued on a faster tier where it cannot overrun the other end's frame: in a voice sub-slot, with the floor free, or where `listen_every >= 2` and the overrun is at most `frame_ms - voice_ms - 20` ms);
3. **control starvation**: if an ARQ chunk is due and at least **1000 ms** have passed since this end's last Listener frame, it sends a Listener frame with that chunk in place of a voice frame (the voice frame is dropped).

A voice frame that may not be sent now (an end that does not hold the floor) is discarded, never sent later. Voice frames are never acknowledged or resent.

### 11.3 Voice buffers (informative for the modem, normative in effect)

A modem holds up to **2** sealed voice frames from its app; a third is refused (VHP VoiceOut is dropped). In push to talk, frames queued while the floor is not this end's are discarded at its next sub-slot. On receipt a modem keeps only the last voice frame heard and hands it to the app once.

### 11.4 Slot frames

**Voice data (sub-type 5)**

| Body offset | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | flags | Table 4 |
| 1 | late | u8: `floor((time this frame starts - slot start) / 4 ms)`, at most 255 |
| 2 | sack | u8: the ARQ SACK byte (V223.LRA.LINK clause 9.3.3) |
| 3 | sealed | the sealed voice frame, 1 to 246 bytes |

**Listener (sub-type 6)**

| Body offset | Field | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | flags | Table 4 |
| 1 | late | u8, as above (0 before the slots) |
| 2 | sack | u8: the ARQ SACK byte |
| 3 | snr | i16: the SNR x4 of the last frame of the call this end heard |
| 5 | seq | u8: present only with flag CHUNK: the ARQ sequence number of the chunk |
| 6 | chunk | present only with flag CHUNK: the ARQ chunk (1 to `chunk(t)` bytes, see clause 11.2) |

Without flag CHUNK the Listener body SHALL be exactly 5 bytes. In both sub-types the header Seq is the slot number modulo 256 once the sender's slots run (0 before), and the header Ack is the sender's ARQ cumulative acknowledgement. Both sub-types count as frames heard (V223.LRA.LINK clause 10) and as SNR samples; their ARQ ack and SACK are processed as an Ack frame, and a chunk is processed as a Data frame (an Ack is owed and is carried by the next slot frame).

**Table 4: Slot frame flags**

| Bit | Mask | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0x01 | READY | The sender's app is ready for voice |
| 1 | 0x02 | SLOTS | The sender's slots run |
| 2 | 0x04 | KEY | The sender's push to talk key is down |
| 3 | 0x08 | CHUNK | A Listener carries an ARQ chunk |
| 5 to 4 | 0x30 | OWNER | Caller frames only: the floor owner, 0 none, 1 caller, 2 answerer. The answerer sends 0 |
| 7 to 6 | 0xC0 | (reserved) | Sent as 0, ignored |

A voice data or Listener frame never carries MORE; in data timing it ends the sender's turn.

### 11.5 Slot timing

- Slot n starts at the caller's time `t(n)` and lasts the plan's frame_ms. The caller's sub-slot is at the start of the slot; the answerer's follows the caller's frame.
- **Hold**: neither end SHALL start a transmission earlier than **20 ms** (the slot guard) after the end of the last frame it heard or sent. After sending in a slot in which the answerer is due, the caller SHALL also keep clear the answerer's sub-slot: it holds until `airtime + 20 + B` ms after the start of its frame, where B is voice_ms (duplex, or the answerer holds the floor) or listen_ms (otherwise).
- **Answerer clock**: on each caller slot frame with flag SLOTS (n in its header Seq), the answerer SHALL set `t(n) = (end of that frame) - air_ms(t, frame length) - 4 * late`, and take the floor owner from its flags.
- The **answerer** sends in its sub-slot once it has heard the caller in this slot; or at once when the caller is not due and `room` is false; or, when it may run on its own clock (duplex, or it holds the floor with its key down), once `voice_ms` (duplex, or the caller holds the floor) or `listen_ms` (otherwise) have passed since t(n).
- In push to talk, while the answerer holds the floor, the caller sends in a slot only if it can start within 20 ms of t(n).
- A sender sends at most one slot frame per slot, and never after its slot has ended.
- If a station's slot clock falls 8 or more slots behind (a stalled tick), the caller starts a new slot at once (n + 1), and the answerer stops sending until a caller frame sets its clock again.
- A tier change takes effect for the plan and Table 5 at the next slot boundary.

### 11.6 Slot layout

**Table 5: Slot layout per tier (normative)**

| Tier | Mode | frame_ms | voice_ms | listen_ms | listen_every | chunk(t) | room | L (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SF5 | Duplex | 120 | 46 | 28 | 1 | 32 | yes | 120 |
| SF6 | Duplex | 200 | 73 | 34 | 1 | 32 | yes | 200 |
| SF7 | Duplex | 200 | 59 | 44 | 1 | 32 | yes | 200 |
| SF8 | Duplex | 200 | 74 | 62 | 1 | 32 | yes | 200 |
| SF9 | PTT | 400 | 144 | 92 | 3 | 32 | yes | 1200 |
| SF10 | PTT | 400 | 246 | 154 | 3 | 32 | no | 1200 |
| SF11 | PTT | 1000 | 595 | 267 | 1 | 32 | yes | 1000 |
| SF12 | PTT delayed | 2000 | 1784 | 432 | 2 | 24 | no | 4000 |

Derivation (for checking; the table governs):

- `listen_ms(c) = air_ms(t, 5 + 1 + 5 + 1 + c) + 20` (a Listener with a c byte chunk, plus the guard).
- `voice_ms = max(air_ms(t, 5 + 1 + 3 + max_sealed) + 20, listen_ms(32))`.
- Duplex: `listen_every = 1`, `chunk = 32`.
- PTT: start with `k = max(1, floor(1500 / frame_ms))`; take the largest c from 32 down to 8 with `k * voice_ms + listen_ms(c) <= k * frame_ms`; if none, increase k (at most 8) and try again. Then `listen_every = k`, `chunk = c`.
- `listen_ms = listen_ms(chunk)`; `room = voice_ms + listen_ms + 20 <= frame_ms`.

### 11.7 The push to talk floor

The caller arbitrates. At the start of every slot (push to talk only) the caller computes the next owner:

1. if it holds the floor and its key is down, it keeps it;
2. else if the answerer holds it and the answerer's key (flag KEY of its last frame) is down, the answerer keeps it;
3. else if the caller's key is down, the caller takes it;
4. else if the answerer's key is down, the answerer takes it;
5. else the floor is free.

A change is announced in the caller's frame of that slot (OWNER bits), and the caller is due in that slot. The answerer SHALL take the owner from the caller's frames. The floor starts free when the slots start and is always Free in duplex. A key down while the other end holds the floor waits until that end lets go.

### 11.8 Timers while the slots run

- A station SHALL NOT send the data timing keepalive PINGs of V223.LRA.LINK clause 10.1.
- **Silence** for step down and resync is counted in due slots only: at the end of each slot in which the peer was due and nothing of the peer was heard, frame_ms is added to the silence and one to the missed count; anything heard resets both. The missed count stands for unanswered PINGs in V223.LRA.LINK clause 10.4, and the silence for the time without hearing in clauses 10.4 and 10.5 (step down at `4 * RTO + off` with 2 missed, resync at `8 * RTO`). Before the answerer's clock is set, wall time counts.
- **Push to talk wall caps.** In push to talk, on top, with q the time since anything of the call was heard: from `q >= 3 L` the station SHALL send a Keepalive with PING (turn ending, sent in its sub-slot) and again every L while unanswered; at `q >= 5 L` with at least 2 PINGs unanswered it SHALL request one tier slower (once per silence); at `q >= 7 L` it SHALL enter resync. These limits do not depend on S22.

**Table 6: Push to talk wall caps**

| Tier | L | PING from | Step down from | Resync from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SF9, SF10 | 1200 ms | 3600 ms | 6000 ms | 8400 ms |
| SF11 | 1000 ms | 3000 ms | 5000 ms | 7000 ms |
| SF12 | 4000 ms | 12000 ms | (none: slowest tier) | 28000 ms |

- Duplex tiers have no wall caps: every slot is due both ways, so the due slot count is wall time. Resync comes at `8 * RTO`: 1496 ms (SF5), 1952 ms (SF6), 2768 ms (SF7), 4256 ms (SF8).
- S22 ends a call only from resync (V223.LRA.LINK clause 10.5). On a call already on SF12, the resync clock starts at the last frame heard, so with S22 below 28 s the call ends with NO CARRIER at 28 s of silence.
- A retrain TierSet waits for the station's sub-slot; while one is queued no second copy is made.
- Leaving resync: the caller starts a new slot at once (slot number plus one); the answerer sends no slot frame until a caller frame sets its clock.

## 12 The voice host protocol (VHP)

The VHP is the interface between a voice app and its modem. It is the app to modem interface of V223.LRA: a voice app written to this clause works with any conforming modem, and a modem written to it works with any conforming app. Version 1.

> **Editor's note.** The modem of r.420 does not check the version byte of Hello and always answers version 1. A later revision will say what a modem does with a Hello of another version (V223.LRA, version negotiation). Apps SHALL send 1.

### 12.1 Transport and framing

- The VHP runs over any reliable, ordered byte stream: the modem's host serial stream (session 0) or a stream the modem's platform provides, such as TCP (sessions 1 to 3). How sessions 1 to 3 are reached is a platform matter.
- **Session 0**: the app sends the AT command line `AT+VOICE` followed by CR, reads the modem's echo and its `OK` (verbose form CR LF `OK` CR LF), and from then on the stream carries VHP frames both ways. A modem that cannot start the VHP answers `ERROR` (during a data or other call, in online command mode). A single LF right after the CR of the AT+VOICE line SHALL be ignored by the modem.
- The app SHOULD begin with a single 0x00 byte, then its first frame (the 0x00 ends anything partial).
- Every frame is COBS encoded [COBS] (code bytes 0x01 to 0xFF, blocks of at most 254 data bytes; a 0xFF code adds no zero) and followed by one 0x00 delimiter. Decoded, a frame is one **type** byte and its **payload**, 1 to 260 bytes in all.
- A frame that does not decode (a zero inside a block, a block cut short by the delimiter, longer than 260 bytes, or empty) SHALL be dropped silently; the next 0x00 starts a fresh frame.
- A frame that decodes but has an unknown type, or a payload of the wrong shape, SHALL be answered with Error (clause 12.5).
- Integers are little endian (u16, i16, u32). A **str** is a length byte and that many bytes (names and labels are printable ASCII). An i16 shown as "none" is -32768 (0x8000).

### 12.2 Sessions

- A modem SHALL support 4 sessions (0 to 3), one registration each.
- A session closes on Leave, when its stream closes, or after **5000 ms** with no good frame from the app. The app SHALL send a frame at least every 2000 ms (Heartbeat when it has nothing else; the reference app sends one after 1500 ms of quiet). A session never times out while the modem holds control bytes for it (clause 12.4, ControlOut).
- On close, a call the session owns is hung up at once. Its registration lives 30000 ms more, except after Leave, which drops it at once. After Leave the modem sends no more frames on that session.
- When session 0 closes, the host stream goes back to AT commands; the modem first prints `NO CARRIER` if the session owned a connected call.
- While session 0 is open, nothing of the AT terminal reaches the host stream, and the modem turns away data callers (it rejects their ring so the caller sees BUSY, or hangs up a call auto answer already took).

### 12.3 Ownership and delivery

- The session that placed a call (Call, or a voice dial from the AT terminal, which uses the first registered session) or answered it **owns** it. Only the owner receives CallState, Floor, VoiceIn, ControlIn and the end of call message, and only the owner may send Hangup, VoiceOut, ControlOut, TalkKey and VoiceReady. Other sessions see CallState idle and Floor free.
- A ringing call goes as Ring to every open session whose registration it rings (clause 9.2), every 3000 ms while it rings. When one of them answers or declines, the others receive AnsweredElsewhere or Declined. When the ring stops with no pickup here, each rung session receives AnsweredElsewhere (another device picked up), Declined (another device declined) or RingEnded (the caller gave up or hung up, or S7 ran out).
- Each session's frames go out one at a time; the modem builds the next when the last has gone, in this priority order: HelloAck, Error, Ring, the end messages (AnsweredElsewhere, Declined, NoAnswer, RingEnded, Ended), CallState, Floor, ControlIn, VoiceIn, AtReply, DirEntry or DirEnd. A session that does not read loses only VoiceIn frames (the modem keeps the last voice frame heard).

### 12.6 Typical flows (informative)

- Start: Hello, HelloAck; Register; then Heartbeat at least every 2 s.
- Call out: Call; CallState dialing; then NoAnswer, Declined or Ended, or CallState training and connected; the apps run the scrambler handshake with ControlOut and ControlIn; each sends VoiceReady; CallState flags bit 0; VoiceOut and VoiceIn (push to talk: TalkKey 1, Floor mine, talk, TalkKey 0). Hangup; Ended; CallState idle.
- Call in: Ring every 3 s; Answer (or Decline); CallState training, connected; the rest as above.

### 12.7 AtLine

- An AtLine runs one AT command line through the modem's own command parser, exactly as its terminal would; the app can thereby run the whole modem (settings, directory, scans).
- The output, as the terminal would show it (CR LF line ends, the modem's numeric or verbose setting), comes back in AtReply frames of at most 240 text bytes; the last has flags bit 0 set and ends with the result code. Listings and scans that finish later (AT+DIR?, AT+SCAN) send their lines as they come, then the result.
- One AtLine at a time per modem: a second, from any session, before the final AtReply receives Error 5.
- The following SHALL be refused with Error 6 (nothing runs, no AtReply): AT+MODE= (any set form), AT+SIMNET= (any set form), AT+MENU, AT+VOICE, ATO, ATA, ATD, ATDT, ATDP, ATDV, AT+FB; and ATH or ATZ while a data call, a non radio call or a bridge is up. Voice calls are placed and answered with Call and Answer.
- Passwords are masked in AtReply (`AT+WAP?` shows `********`); the line is never echoed.

> **Editor's note.** The AT command set itself is an LFD3K-SM implementation feature and is not standardised by V223.LRA. An app that relies on AtLine depends on the modem's command set; a future part may standardise a minimal set.

## Annex D (informative): What the two applications say to each other

The modem carries the apps' control bytes as an opaque reliable stream. V223.LRA.92 specifies what the reference apps send on it (framing of control messages, the scrambler handshake, sealed key confirm, mute, device removal and device linking) and the sealed voice frame. In short, as the reference app runs a call:

1. CallState connected: the caller sends its commit; the answerer sends its hello only after the commit; the caller reveals; each side sends a sealed Hello.
2. When the peer's sealed Hello opens, the app shows the two words and the trust verdict; audio is held both ways until the verdict is Checked or the user accepts the words or chooses to talk anyway.
3. Then removals (if any) and VoiceReady. Voice starts once both apps are ready (CallState flags bit 0).
4. The reference app works on 16 kHz mono audio internally (Codec 2 runs at 8 kHz behind a 2 to 1 resampler), uses a jitter buffer 2 frames deep in duplex and 1 in push to talk, and conceals up to 4 lost frames in a row in the middle of speech.

Control bytes are slow once the slots run (one Listener chunk of up to 32 bytes per listener slot, 24 bytes every 4 s at SF12), so apps send them only when needed.

## Annex E (informative): Rationale and measured timings

### E.1 How the talk plan was chosen

A tier is duplex only where both voice frames on the air plus the 20 ms slot guard after each leave at least 20 % of the frame free: `2 * (air_ms(t, 9 + sealed) + 20) <= 0.8 * frame_ms` (`duplex_fits`). Each duplex tier takes its first choice that fits: SF5 Opus 10 kbit/s in 120 ms (12 kbit/s in 100 ms does not fit), else Opus 8 kbit/s in 120 ms, else Codec 2 3200 in 200 ms; SF6 Opus 8 kbit/s in 200 ms, else Codec 2 3200; SF7 Codec 2 2400; SF8 Codec 2 1300 in 200 ms. SF9 and slower are push to talk. Sealed frames stay within the 246 bytes a voice data frame can carry.

### E.2 Why slots

Data timing (bursts, turn gaps, carrier sense) wastes a voice call's airtime on turn gaps and makes the delay vary. Fixed slots with the caller as clock master give each end a known moment to talk, with only a 20 ms guard, and let the push to talk listener get a word in every listen period.

### E.3 Measured in the simulator (r.420, voice-1 branch)
