## Foreword

This specification is an AXIS / LFD3K-SM specification, published by Rev. J. Money. It is not an ITU-T Recommendation.

The LORA FLOWTRON DATAMASTER 3000 SERIAL MODEM (LFD3K-SM) is a Hayes style modem that dials other modems by name over LoRa radio in the 902 to 928 MHz band, trains to the fastest speed the path allows, and carries a reliable byte stream (for example a caller into a bulletin board system such as AXIS) or, between two voice applications, an encrypted voice call. V223.LRA is the name of the whole protocol stack it speaks. This umbrella part describes the architecture, names the parts and the conformance classes, and sets the rules that let the stack grow without breaking stations already in the field.

The parts were written from the LFD3K-SM reference implementation so that anyone can build a station or an application that interoperates with it, from the documents alone. **Release r.420 of LFD3K-SM implements V223.LRA Revision 1.0.**

## Implementing this standard

Independent implementations are welcome: a second modem on other hardware, a voice application for another platform, a gateway, a test tool. Anyone may implement V223.LRA and its parts freely and without royalty (see the license above and clause 13). An implementation may claim conformance to a part or to a class per the conformance clauses of the parts and clause 9 of this part; the use of the V223.LRA name for a product follows clause 14.

## 1 Scope

This part:

- describes the architecture of an LFD3K-SM system (stations, hosts and voice applications) and the layer model of the stack;
- lists the parts and how they fit together;
- defines conformance classes D (data), V (voice) and S (scrambler);
- describes how stations signal capabilities and versions, and what an implementation must do to interoperate with LFD3K-SM r.420 and later;
- sets the revision policy and the registries for every number the parts allocate;
- gives informative regulatory notes.

The technical requirements are in the parts.

## 6 Architecture

```
  host (terminal, BBS)        voice app A                          voice app B
        |  AT commands, data        |  VHP (COBS frames)                 |  VHP
        |                           |                                    |
  +=====+===========================+=====+                    +=========+======+
  |            station (modem)            |  LoRa, 918.5 MHz   |  station       |
  |  data link, ARQ, compression, voice   | <================> |  (modem)       |
  |  scheduler, discovery                 |   V223.LRA.LINK    |                |
  +=======================================+   V223.LRA.90      +================+

  app A <====== FLOWTRON SCRAMBLER (V223.LRA.92), end to end, through both modems ======> app B
```

- **Stations** talk to each other over one LoRa channel (V223.LRA.LINK). A data call carries a reliable byte stream between two hosts. A voice call carries sealed voice frames and the apps' control bytes.
- **Voice apps** talk to their own station over the VHP (V223.LRA.90 clause 12): session 0 over the station's host stream, or sessions 1 to 3 over a platform link such as TCP. Up to four apps (four people or devices) can share one station.
- **The scrambler** runs only between the two apps. Stations carry opaque bytes; they never hold keys or audio.

## 10 Capability signalling and version negotiation

V223.LRA evolves in five places. Each is designed so that an older implementation ignores what it does not know.

1. **Air identifier (byte 0 of every frame).** 0xD1 = LFD3K-SM family (0xD), air protocol version 1. A station SHALL treat a frame with any other first byte as foreign and ignore it silently. An incompatible change to the frame format would use a new version nibble (0xD2 and on); stations of version 1 would not see such frames at all. There is no negotiation of the air version in Revision 1.0.
2. **Capability byte** (CallReq, CallAck, Train; V223.LRA.LINK Table 6). Bit 0 is CAP_LZ. Bits 1 to 3 are reserved for future capabilities: senders set them to 0 and receivers ignore them, so they can be allocated later without breaking anyone. Bits 4 to 7 make a CallReq or Train malformed in r.420 (a voice call request masks them instead); they SHALL NOT be allocated in version 1. A capability is in force when both ends offered it; the answerer states the agreement and the caller reads it from the Train.
3. **Frame type 15 sub-types** (V223.LRA.90 Table 1). Frame types 1 to 15 are all allocated, so new functions arrive as type 15 sub-types. A station SHALL drop a type 15 frame with a sub-type it does not implement, so a new sub-type is invisible to an older station.
4. **Flags and bit fields.** Header flags are defined per frame type (V223.LRA.LINK Table 5); an undefined flag is sent as 0 and ignored. The same holds for services bits 4 to 7 and slot frame flag bits 6 and 7.
5. **Version bytes above the air.** VHP Hello carries a protocol version (1); the scrambler hello, link messages and cert labels carry version 1. A receiver of a hello or link message with another version fails that exchange. The r.420 modem does not yet check the VHP Hello version (Editor's note in V223.LRA.90 clause 12).

Builds older than r.420 refuse a CallReq that carries a capability byte; a station calling such a build sends none (LFD3K-SM: S31 = 0). Older builds drop discovery frames (types 12 to 14) and every type 15 frame.

## 11 The interoperability layer

To interoperate with LFD3K-SM r.420 and every later release that implements V223.LRA Revision 1.x, an implementation:

1. SHALL use exactly the radio parameters, tier table and time on air formula of V223.LRA.LINK clause 6, and derive its timers from them, so that both ends of a call compute the same timeouts;
2. SHALL send only frames whose layouts are given in the parts, byte for byte, and SHALL drop frames whose shape is not exactly as given;
3. SHALL ignore foreign frames, undefined flags, reserved capability bits and unknown type 15 sub-types;
4. SHALL follow the call setup, training, ARQ, retrain and resync rules in both roles, including the answerer's added waits that keep the two ends out of step;
5. SHALL obey the discovery air manners (SF12, no call, listen before talk, one discovery frame per 10 s) and the privacy mode it claims;
6. if it offers compression, SHALL decode exactly per V223.LRA.CMPRS and never change the preset;
7. for voice, SHALL derive the talk plan and slot layout from the tier with the normative tables of V223.LRA.90 (they are never negotiated) and follow the scheduler;
8. for apps, SHALL implement the VHP and the scrambler exactly, including the counter shared by voice frames and sealed control messages;
9. SHALL pass the test vectors of every part it claims.

## 12 Revision and registration policy

### 12.1 Revisions

- Each part has a revision number `major.minor` and, before publication, a draft number (D1, D2 and on). This set starts at Revision 1.0 (draft D1).
- A **minor** revision only adds: new registry entries, new optional features signalled by a capability bit or a new type 15 sub-type, clarifications. A station of revision 1.x SHALL interoperate with a station of revision 1.y for any x and y.
- A **major** revision may change frame formats; it changes the air identifier version nibble or the relevant version byte.
- Each LFD3K-SM release states which revision of V223.LRA it implements. r.420 implements Revision 1.0.

### 12.2 Registries

The registries of Annex A hold every number the parts allocate. Rules:

- The editor allocates numbers. A request names the part, the purpose, the frames or messages involved and a reference implementation or a draft.
- A number, once published, SHALL NOT be given another meaning. Retired numbers stay reserved.
- New functions SHALL use the extension points of clause 10: type 15 sub-types, capability bits 1 to 3, flag bits defined per frame type, VHP message types, codec ids, control message types.
- The preset dictionary of V223.LRA.CMPRS, a talk plan entry of V223.LRA.90 Table 2, and the labels of V223.LRA.92 SHALL NOT change within a major revision without a new capability bit, sub-type or version byte.
- Ranges marked "experimental" in Annex A may be used by anyone for testing without registration and SHALL NOT be sent by a product on the air.

## Annex B (informative): Regulatory notes

These notes are for orientation only. **They are not legal advice.** Every operator and builder is responsible for the rules where they operate.

- **United States, 47 CFR 15.247 (digital modulation, 902 to 928 MHz).** The 6 dB bandwidth must be at least 500 kHz; this is why every tier of V223.LRA.LINK uses BW 500 kHz and no frequency hopping is needed. Peak conducted output power up to 1 W (30 dBm), reduced by 1 dB for each dB of antenna gain above 6 dBi; a power spectral density limit of 8 dBm in any 3 kHz band applies. Part 15.247 digital modulation has no duty cycle limit; the air manners of the parts still keep the channel shared. The rules govern the emission, not its content: digital voice is data to the radio, and encryption is allowed under Part 15.
- **Amateur radio.** 902 to 928 MHz is also the 33 cm amateur band. Amateur rules (in the US, 47 CFR 97.113(a)(4)) prohibit messages encoded to obscure their meaning, and require station identification. A future ham mode of LFD3K-SM would send a callsign, disable the FLOWTRON SCRAMBLER (calls in the clear, clearly shown) and allow higher power. Ham mode is not part of Revision 1.0.
- **Other countries.** 918.5 MHz is not available for licence free use everywhere (for example, Europe uses 863 to 870 MHz with duty cycle limits). V223.LRA Revision 1.0 defines only the 918.5 MHz channel; a regional channel plan would be a later registry entry.
