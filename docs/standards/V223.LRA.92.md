## Foreword

This specification is an AXIS / LFD3K-SM specification, published by Rev. J. Money as part of the V223.LRA protocol stack. It is not an ITU-T Recommendation.

The FLOWTRON SCRAMBLER is how two LFD3K-SM voice apps make a call private. It uses only standard, well studied parts: X25519, Ed25519, SHA-256, HKDF-SHA256, HMAC-SHA256, ChaCha20-Poly1305 and SPAKE2. Nothing in it is a homemade cipher. The modems in between carry only opaque bytes and never see a key or a sample of audio. Each call gets fresh keys, both users can compare two spoken words to rule out a man in the middle, and the app remembers whom the user has checked.

## 1 Scope

This part specifies, for voice apps (not modems): the long term device and person keys and the device certificate; the framing of the control messages two apps exchange through the call's reliable stream (V223.LRA.90, ControlOut and ControlIn); the call handshake; the derivation of the session keys and of the two words; the sealed frame format used for voice frames and sealed control messages, with its nonce and replay rules; key confirmation, mute and device removal messages; the trust rules; and linking a person's devices with a 6 digit code.

## 3 Terms and definitions

- **3.1 app**: a voice application (V223.LRA.90 clause 3.3).
- **3.2 device key**: an Ed25519 key pair made once per app install. It identifies one device.
- **3.3 person key**: an Ed25519 key pair that stands for a person across devices. It is made by the person's first device and copied to later devices by linking (clause 15).
- **3.4 device certificate (cert)**: a statement, signed by a person key, that a device key belongs to that person's voice name and device label.
- **3.5 hello**: the signed bundle each side contributes to the handshake (clause 9.1).
- **3.6 caller, answerer**: the app on the side of the call that dialed, or that answered. The roles come from the call (V223.LRA.90 CallState flags bit 1).
- **3.7 session keys**: the two 32 byte ChaCha20-Poly1305 keys of one call, one per direction.
- **3.8 sealed**: encrypted and authenticated per clause 12.
- **3.9 the two words**: two words from the PGP word list that both users read aloud to check the call.
- **3.10 verdict**: Checked, NotChecked or Conflict (clause 14).
- **3.11 link code**: a 6 digit decimal number shown on one device and typed on another to link them.

## 5 Conventions

BCP 14 keywords [RFC2119] [RFC8174] apply when in capitals. `||` here means byte string concatenation (written as a symbol, it is not prose). Text in double quotes in a formula is its ASCII bytes with no terminator; Annex A lists every such label in hexadecimal. Integers on the wire are little endian. A **field** is one length byte and that many bytes. Keys and signatures are in the encodings of their RFCs (32 byte X25519 public keys and secrets, 32 byte Ed25519 public keys and seeds, 64 byte Ed25519 signatures).

## 6 Overview

1. Each app has a device key; each person has a person key that signs a cert for each of their devices.
2. When a voice call connects, the two apps run a three message handshake over the call's reliable stream: the caller **commits** to its hello, the answerer sends its **hello**, the caller **reveals** its hello. Each hello holds a fresh X25519 key, the device key, the cert, and a signature by the device key.
3. Both derive two session keys with X25519 and HKDF, and the two words from a hash of both hellos.
4. Each side sends a sealed Hello as key confirmation. When the peer's opens, the app shows the two words and its trust verdict, holds audio until the user's trust decision, and then tells the modem it is ready (V223.LRA.90 VoiceReady).
5. Voice frames and further control messages are sealed with the session keys.

The scrambler runs only between the apps. The modems and the air never carry a key in the clear.

> **Ham mode.** On amateur radio frequencies, encryption that hides meaning is not allowed (V223.LRA, informative regulatory notes). An app in a ham mode SHALL NOT use this part: calls go in the clear and the app says so. Ham mode does not exist in r.420; this is the rule for when it does.

## 7 Keys and device certificates

### 7.1 Keys

- An app SHALL make one Ed25519 device key from 32 bytes of a cryptographically secure random source when it is first set up, and keep it for the life of the install.
- The person's first device SHALL also make an Ed25519 person key the same way and sign its own device key with it (clause 7.2). Later devices get the person key by linking (clause 15).
- Private keys SHALL be stored where only the user can read them and SHALL never be shown, logged or sent, except the person key inside the sealed transfer of clause 15.

### 7.2 Device certificate

The person key signs, with Ed25519 [RFC8032], the **cert message**:

`"LFD3K CERT v1" || 0x20 || device_pub (32) || len(name) || name || len(label) || label`

where name is the voice name and label the device label (V223.LRA.90 clause 3). Each of name and label SHALL be at most 64 bytes (an app makes them per V223.LRA.90: 1 to 16 and 1 to 8 bytes); a cert with a longer field is invalid.

The **cert on the wire** (inside a hello) leaves out the device key, which the hello already carries:

| Offset | Field | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | person_pub | 32 |
| 32 | name | field (length byte, 0 to 64 bytes) |
| . | label | field (length byte, 0 to 64 bytes) |
| . | sig | 64: the person key's signature over the cert message |

A cert is **valid** when its fields are within the limits and `sig` verifies (clause 7.3) under `person_pub` over the cert message built with the hello's device key.

### 7.3 Signature verification

Every Ed25519 verification in this part SHALL be strict: reject a signature whose S is not below the group order L, reject non-canonical encodings of the public key or of R, and reject a public key or R of small order. (This is the `verify_strict` rule of the ed25519-dalek library used by the reference app.)

## 8 Control messages

The two apps of a call exchange **control messages** over the call's reliable, ordered byte stream (VHP ControlOut and ControlIn, V223.LRA.90 clause 12). Because the far modem may cut the stream at any byte, every message is framed:

`length (u16, little endian) || type (1 byte) || body`, where length counts the type byte and the body.

- A length of 0 or above **1024** means the stream is broken: the receiver SHALL stop reading control messages for the rest of the call and keep the call muted.
- A message of an unknown type SHALL be skipped.

**Table 1: Control message types**

| Type | Name | Body | Clause |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x01 | Commit | 32 bytes | 9 |
| 0x02 | Hello | answerer hello | 9 |
| 0x03 | Reveal | caller hello | 9 |
| 0x10 | Link request | 0x01 (version) | 15 |
| 0x11 | Link offer | 0x01, SPAKE2 message (33) | 15 |
| 0x12 | Link join | 0x01, SPAKE2 message (33), join confirm (32) | 15 |
| 0x13 | Link sealed | 0x01, offer confirm (32), sealed person key and name | 15 |
| 0x20 | Sealed Hello | sealed `"FLOWTRON HELLO v1"` | 13.1 |
| 0x21 | Mute | sealed 1 byte: 1 muted, 0 not | 13.2 |
| 0x22 | Remove device | sealed: device key (32), person signature (64) | 13.3 |
| others | (not allocated here) | | Ranges in V223.LRA Annex A.10 |

Ranges 0x01 to 0x0F (handshake), 0x10 to 0x1F (linking) and 0x20 to 0x2F (sealed call messages) are kept apart so one stream carries them all.

## 9 The call handshake (commit, then reveal)

### 9.1 The hello

| Offset | Field | Size | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | version | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | role | 1 | 0 caller, 1 answerer |
| 2 | eph_pub | 32 | X25519 public key of a fresh ephemeral secret [RFC7748], made for this call only |
| 34 | device_pub | 32 | The sender's Ed25519 device public key |
| 66 | cert flag | 1 | 0 no cert; 1 a cert follows |
| 67 | cert | var | Clause 7.2, present only with cert flag 1 |
| . | sig | 64 | Ed25519 signature by the device key over every byte of the hello before it |

The message type byte that carries a hello on the stream (0x02 or 0x03) is not part of the hello: it is outside the signature, the commit, the HKDF salt and the words hash.

A receiver SHALL accept a peer hello only if: version is 1; role is the peer's role; the cert flag is 0 or 1; the hello ends exactly after the signature; the signature verifies under device_pub (clause 7.3); and, with a cert, the cert is valid for device_pub (clause 7.2). Otherwise the handshake fails and the call stays muted.

### 9.2 Sequence

1. When the call reaches CallState connected, each app makes a fresh X25519 secret and its hello.
2. The **caller** sends **Commit** with body `SHA-256("FLOWTRON COMMIT" || caller hello)`.
3. The **answerer** SHALL NOT send its hello before it has received the Commit. It then sends **Hello** (type 0x02) with its hello.
4. The caller checks the answerer hello (clause 9.1) and sends **Reveal** (type 0x03) with its own hello.
5. The answerer SHALL check that `SHA-256("FLOWTRON COMMIT" || revealed hello)` equals the committed value, then checks the caller hello (clause 9.1).

Each side accepts only one message of each kind; any other message, a second copy, a message out of this order, a Commit body that is not 32 bytes, a commit mismatch or a bad hello fails the handshake (the call stays muted). The caller SHALL derive its keys from the same answerer hello it checked.

## 10 Key derivation

1. `shared = X25519(own ephemeral secret, peer eph_pub)` [RFC7748]. If `shared` is all zero bytes (a non-contributory peer key) the handshake fails.
2. `salt = caller hello || answerer hello`.
3. `PRK = HKDF-Extract(salt, shared)` with SHA-256 [RFC5869].
4. `k_c2a = HKDF-Expand(PRK, "FLOWTRON SCRAMBLER v1 c2a", 32)` (caller to answerer) and `k_a2c = HKDF-Expand(PRK, "FLOWTRON SCRAMBLER v1 a2c", 32)` (answerer to caller).

The caller seals with `k_c2a` and direction byte 0 and opens with `k_a2c` and direction 1; the answerer the reverse. The ephemeral secret, `shared` and the session keys SHALL be wiped when the call ends.

## 11 The two words

`W = SHA-256("FLOWTRON WORDS" || caller hello || answerer hello)`. Word 1 is entry `W[0]` of the even list and word 2 entry `W[1]` of the odd list (Annex D). Both apps SHALL show the two words, in that order (apps may show them in capitals). Users compare them by voice; if they match, no one is in the middle.

## 12 Sealed frames, nonces and the replay window

### 12.1 Format

A **sealed frame** is `counter (u32, little endian) || ciphertext || tag (16)`, where ciphertext and tag are AEAD_CHACHA20_POLY1305 [RFC8439] of the plaintext under the sender's session key, with the 12 byte nonce

`direction (1 byte: 0 caller to answerer, 1 answerer to caller) || seven 0x00 bytes || counter (u32, little endian)`

and **empty associated data**. A sealed frame is 20 bytes longer than its plaintext.

### 12.2 Counter

Each direction of a call has **one** counter, shared by every sealed frame that direction sends: voice frames (V223.LRA.90 clause 10.3) and sealed control messages (types 0x20 to 0x22) alike. It starts at 0 and increases by one with each sealed frame. A counter value SHALL never be used twice under one key; when the counter would pass `2^32 - 1` the sender SHALL stop sending and end the call.

### 12.3 Opening and the replay window

A receiver keeps, per call, the highest counter it has opened (none at first) and a 64 bit window of the counters it has opened at and below it. It SHALL drop a sealed frame that is shorter than 20 bytes, whose counter is at or below `highest - 64`, whose counter it has already opened within the window, or that fails AEAD verification. A frame that opens updates the highest counter and the window. Dropped frames are not an error (voice is lossy).

Sealed control messages (types 0x20 to 0x22) travel in the call's reliable, ordered control stream (V223.LRA.90 clause 11), so one may arrive long after voice frames sealed after it; the window above SHALL NOT be applied to them. A receiver keeps, per call, the counter of the last sealed control message it opened (none at first) and SHALL drop a sealed control message that is shorter than 20 bytes, whose counter is at or below that last one (the stream is in order, so it is a replay), whose counter it has already opened within the window, that fails AEAD verification, or whose plaintext does not have the shape of the message it came in (clause 13: exactly the Sealed Hello bytes, 1 byte for Mute, 96 bytes for Remove device; so an old voice frame fed in neither opens as control nor moves the last control counter on). One that opens becomes the last control counter and, when it lies within the window (or above the highest counter), updates the highest counter and the window as a voice frame does, so it can never be opened again as a voice frame.

## 13 Key confirmation, mute and device removal

### 13.1 Sealed Hello (0x20)

As soon as its handshake finishes, each side SHALL send a Sealed Hello whose body is the sealed frame of the 17 bytes `"FLOWTRON HELLO v1"`. The peer is **live** only once its Sealed Hello opens to exactly those bytes. Before then an app SHALL NOT play received audio, show the words as checked, or apply the peer's other sealed messages.

### 13.2 Mute (0x21)

The body is a sealed frame of one byte: 1 when the sender's microphone is muted, 0 when not. An app sends it when its mute state changes, and at the start of a call if it is already muted.

### 13.3 Remove device (0x22)

The body is a sealed frame of 96 bytes: the removed device's public key (32), then the person key's Ed25519 signature (64) over `"LFD3K REMOVE v1" || removed device_pub`. An app sends one for each of its person's devices the user has removed, after its trust decision and before VoiceReady. A receiver SHALL apply it (clause 14) only if the peer is live, the peer's verdict is Checked, the peer sent a cert, and the signature verifies under that cert's person key.

## 14 Trust rules

### 14.1 Contacts

An app keeps a store of **contacts**. A contact is a voice name (compared without regard to case) with: a set of checked device keys, each recorded under the device label it was checked with; at most one checked person key; and a set of removed device keys.

### 14.2 Verdict

When the peer becomes live, the app SHALL compute the verdict for the name the user sees (on a call placed, the voice name dialed; on a call answered, the caller's voice name) and the peer device label, in this order:

1. no contact of that name: **NotChecked**;
2. the peer device key is among the contact's removed keys: **Conflict**;
3. the peer device key is among the contact's checked keys: **Checked**;
4. the peer sent a cert whose person key is the contact's checked person key (and whose device key is the peer device key): **Checked**;
5. a checked device of the contact with the same label (ignoring case) has a different key: **Conflict**;
6. otherwise: **NotChecked**.

A verdict of Checked SHALL be lowered to NotChecked when the peer's cert names a voice name other than the name the user sees (ignoring case).

### 14.3 What the user sees and when audio flows

- **Checked**: show the name; audio may flow.
- **NotChecked**: show the name, label and the two words marked NOT CHECKED. Audio SHALL be held both ways (nothing sent, nothing played) until the user says the words match (the app then records the device key under the label and, if the peer sent a cert, its person key: clause 14.4) or chooses to talk anyway for this call only.
- **Conflict**: audio SHALL stay muted with a loud warning (`NOT THE <NAME> YOU CHECKED`).

Only after this decision does the app send any removals and VoiceReady (V223.LRA.90 clause 11.1).

### 14.4 Recording a check

When the user says the words match, the app SHALL, unless the key is among the contact's removed keys, record the peer device key under the peer label (replacing any key recorded under the same label and any other record of the same key), and, if the peer sent a cert, record the cert's person key as the contact's person key.

### 14.5 Removal

Applying a removal SHALL drop the device key from the contact's checked keys and add it to the removed keys. A removal is permanent: checking the words again never brings a removed key back, because a lost device still holds its key and still shows the right words.

## 15 Linking a person's devices

### 15.1 Purpose and roles

Linking gives a new device (the **joiner**, "Link to my name") the person key and voice name held by an existing device (the **offer**, "Link a device"). The joiner keeps its own device key and label and gets a new cert signed by the person key. Linking runs inside a voice call between the two devices, over control messages.

### 15.2 The code

The offer shows a **link code**: 6 decimal digits, uniform over 000000 to 999999, from a cryptographically secure source (the reference app draws a u32, rejects values of 4294000000 or more, and takes the value modulo 1000000). A code SHALL be used for at most one link run and SHALL expire 300000 ms after it was shown. The joiner's user types it (spaces around it ignored). The code's ASCII digits are the SPAKE2 password.

### 15.3 Messages

Every link message is a control message (clause 8) whose body starts with the version byte 0x01.

| Type | Sent by | Body after the version byte |
|---|---|---|
| 0x10 Link request | joiner | nothing: "I have a code, send your offer" |
| 0x11 Link offer | offer | the offer's SPAKE2 message (33 bytes: 0x41 then a 32 byte point) |
| 0x12 Link join | joiner | the joiner's SPAKE2 message (33 bytes: 0x42 then a point), then the join confirm (32) |
| 0x13 Link sealed | offer | the offer confirm (32), then the sealed transfer (49 to 113 bytes) |

Sequence: the joiner sends the request once the call is live; the offer answers with the offer message; the joiner answers with the join message; the offer checks the join confirm and only then sends the sealed message; the joiner checks the offer confirm and opens the transfer.

### 15.4 Keys

- The offer runs SPAKE2 as side A with identity `"LFD3K LINK OFFER"`, the joiner as side B with identity `"LFD3K LINK JOIN"`, both with the code as password, as Annex B specifies. Both obtain the same 32 byte key K only if both used the same code.
- `PRK = HKDF-Extract("LFD3K LINK v1", K)`; `join_mac_key = HKDF-Expand(PRK, "LFD3K LINK v1 join confirm", 32)`; `offer_mac_key = HKDF-Expand(PRK, "LFD3K LINK v1 offer confirm", 32)`; `seal_key = HKDF-Expand(PRK, "LFD3K LINK v1 seal", 32)`.
- `transcript = 0x01 || offer SPAKE2 message (33) || join SPAKE2 message (33)`.
- `join confirm = HMAC-SHA256(join_mac_key, "JOIN" || transcript)`; `offer confirm = HMAC-SHA256(offer_mac_key, "OFFER" || transcript)` [RFC2104]. Both are compared in constant time.
- The sealed transfer is AEAD_CHACHA20_POLY1305 under `seal_key` with a nonce of 12 zero bytes (the key is used once) and associated data `0x13 || 0x01 || transcript`, of the plaintext `person key seed (32) || len(name) || name` (name at most 64 bytes).

### 15.5 Rules

- The offer SHALL NOT send the sealed message unless the join confirm checks. A wrong code therefore yields nothing sealed; the guesser learns nothing and has spent the code.
- The joiner SHALL check the offer confirm before opening the transfer, and SHALL reject a sealed message shorter than 83 or longer than 147 bytes (with its type and version bytes), a transfer that fails to open, or a name whose length does not match.
- On success the joiner SHALL adopt the person key and the name and replace its cert with a new one for its own device key, the adopted name and its own label (clause 7.2), and save its identity. Nothing changes on any failure.
- A joiner that receives no sealed message within 60000 ms plus 30 voice frames of the current plan after its request SHALL give up (`link failed`); the user may start again with a new code.
- The SPAKE2 secrets, K, the derived keys and the plaintext SHALL be wiped after use.

## 16 Security considerations (informative)

- **Commit then reveal.** Without the commit, an attacker in the middle who saw the answerer's hello first could try many caller hellos until the 16 bits of the two words matched on both sides. The commit fixes the caller's hello before the answerer's is known.
- **The two words** carry 16 bits: an attacker in the middle succeeds with probability 1 in 65536 per call, and only if the users compare the words.
- **Forward secrecy.** Session keys come from ephemeral X25519 keys, so a later theft of device or person keys does not open recorded calls.
- **Replay.** Counters and the 64 frame window stop replayed voice or control frames within a call; keys are fresh per call.
- **What is not hidden.** Station names, voice names, device labels, call timing and frame sizes travel in the clear on the air (V223.LRA.LINK, V223.LRA.90). The scrambler protects content, not metadata.
- **No associated data.** Sealed frames authenticate only their counter (through the nonce) and plaintext. The air header and slot fields are not authenticated; tampering with them can drop or delay audio but cannot forge it.
- **Linking** spends one code per guess; codes are single use and expire after 5 minutes.

## 17 Conformance

An app conforms to V223.LRA.92 if it implements clauses 7 to 14 completely and passes every vector of Annex C that does not involve linking. Linking (clause 15) is OPTIONAL; an app that offers it SHALL implement clause 15 and Annex B completely and pass the linking vectors. A conformance claim SHALL state the revision of this part and whether linking is included. V223.LRA Class S refers to this clause.

## Annex B (normative): The SPAKE2 variant

This is the SPAKE2 of the python `spake2` package (ParamsEd25519) and of the RustCrypto `spake2` crate version 0.5 (asymmetric mode, Ed25519Group). It is **not** RFC 9382: the constants M and N, the password scalar, the message format and the key derivation all differ, and an RFC 9382 implementation will not interoperate.

> **Editor's note.** A future revision may move to RFC 9382 under a new link version byte. Link version 1 is the variant below.

**B.1 Group.** The prime order subgroup of edwards25519 [RFC8032] with base point B and order L = 2^252 + 27742317777372353535851937790883648493. An element is encoded as its 32 byte compressed form (RFC 8032 section 5.1.2); decoding follows RFC 8032 section 5.1.3 and fails for a string that is not a valid encoding. The reference makes no further check (small order, identity); an implementation SHALL NOT add a check that rejects messages the reference accepts.

**B.2 Constants.**

- M = decode(`15cfd18e385952982b6a8f8c7854963b58e34388c8e6dae891db756481a02312`)
- N = decode(`f04f2e7eb734b2a8f8b472eaf9c3c632576ac64aea650b496a8a20ff00e583c3`)
- Password scalar: `OKM = HKDF-SHA256(salt = empty string, IKM = password, info = "SPAKE2 pw", length = 48)`; w is OKM read as a **big endian** integer, reduced modulo L.

**B.3 Messages.**

- Side A draws a uniform scalar x modulo L (the reference reduces 64 random bytes, read little endian, modulo L), computes X = x B + w M and sends `0x41 || encode(X)` (33 bytes).
- Side B draws y likewise, computes Y = y B + w N and sends `0x42 || encode(Y)`.

**B.4 Finishing.** A received message SHALL be exactly 33 bytes, its first byte SHALL be the other side's (A accepts only 0x42, B only 0x41), and its point SHALL decode; otherwise the run fails. Then:

- side A: K = x (Y - w N); side B: K = y (X - w M);
- `key = SHA-256( SHA-256(password) || SHA-256(idA) || SHA-256(idB) || encode(X) || encode(Y) || encode(K) )`,

always with A's point before B's, whichever side computes it. This 32 byte key is the K of clause 15.4.
